# Critical analysis: Sonnet 4.6 vs Sonnet 5.5 (Fable 5.1, 2026-10-12)

Read-only pass over `evals/README.md`, `prompt-variants/sonnet55/CHANGES.md`, `report-chain.md`, the three `score-*.txt`, every `calls.jsonl`, `deterministic.json`, the public judge files and the owner-case judge and deterministic files (read for numbers, nothing quoted). The identical-inputs judge (`*__on-sonnet46-api`) is incomplete (113 of 140 owner passes, no synthetic) and the Codex re-judge has only packets and a brief, no verdicts. Both are left out. Latency reps are also incomplete (4.6 has 4 kits at concurrency 1, all owner cases), so latency below uses the quality pass at concurrency 4, which is like for like.

## 1. Fabrication claims: what survives a manual check

**Judge quotes, 4.6 (checked 30 across cover letter, CV, email on syn-01..16).** About 70% are real: invented outcomes bolted onto real bullets ("with high availability and fault tolerance", "coordinating architecture decisions, code reviews, and phased production rollouts" on syn-07's CV), whole bullets with no source ("Collaborated directly with client stakeholders to gather requirements" at Brightpath), "HubSpot Content Marketing Certification" for a course, "Proficient in Google Analytics" for "basic", "PostgreSQL is also a tool I use daily", "IT-Affinität" (syn-16, not in the CV), and one number conflation that matters: syn-14's letter says accuracy rose "from 88% to 96% across an 85 million USD business unit" when the CV gives 85M as two units combined and the gain for the larger one only. About 30% are inference or judge over-reach: employer locations copied from the applicant's city, "five years in FP&A" for a CV that says 5 years of budgeting and forecasting, "leading design discussions" for someone who led a 4-engineer migration, "three years" for Sep 2022 to Jun 2025.

**Judge quotes, 5.5 (checked all 6 + 16 + 12 low, 12 + 25 + 14 medium).** About 40% real, all mild: "Taught KS3 and KS4 science to mixed-ability classes", "PostgreSQL and Datadog in daily use" for someone on a career break, "Advanced in Excel, Power BI and SAP reporting" (only Excel is advanced), "Supported a small team of 14 staff" (14 is the shop's headcount). The rest are the same location inferences and start-date or availability lines in emails ("can start with four weeks' notice", "free for a call after 4pm UK time"), which are invented but are logistics, not qualifications. No 5.5 output on a synthetic case invents a role, employer, degree, certification, tool or number.

**True rate estimate (synthetic, per kit, substantive fabrications only):** 4.6 roughly 8 to 10, concentrated in the CV (every 4.6 CV has several); 5.5 roughly 1 to 2, mostly availability lines in emails. The headline "16 vs 5 to 6" overstates both because the judge counts location fields and rephrasings, but the ratio holds and the gap in kind (4.6 invents qualifications, 5.5 invents logistics) is bigger than the ratio.

**Deterministic F1/F2/F3/F4 are mostly scorer noise.** F1 is 79 findings in every arm and nearly all are "12" and "2026" (the letter date) and the fit percentage; it carries no signal. F2 on synthetic cases is "Bachelor of Arts" for "B.A." and "Full-Time Family Caregiver" as a title for the career break; 5.5-low has zero real F2 and medium has one ("Family caregiver (planned career break)"). On owner cases the 5.5 F2/F3 findings are degree equivalents and German translations of English skill names, not inventions. F3 on synthetic cases is real for 4.6 (18 added skills such as "Cross-functional collaboration", "Neukundenakquise", "Eigeninitiative") and zero for both 5.5 arms. **F4 bait inverts the headline:** 5.5 trips F4 on 12 to 13 kits because it names the gap ("I haven't worked in Marketo yet", "My background is in Kotlin, Java and Python rather than Go or Rust"). Not one 5.5 F4 hit is a claim. 4.6's F4 hits are mostly honest too, with one soft adjacency claim ("a practical foothold into the data layer the Egress team relies on alongside ClickHouse and Grafana"). Neither model claimed a bait skill. The real bait weakness is upstream: all three arms mark "Experience with Marketing Automation Platforms (Marketo, Eloqua, HubSpot...)" as claimable for syn-02 on Mailchimp or a HubSpot course evidence. That is the unchanged `skill_matching` prompt, not the writer.

## 2. Judge bias and the prompt confound

The rubric says "an output with any fabrication scores 1 on grounding, outright" and "a fabrication normally decides the verdict". 4.6's CV scored grounding 1 in all 32 synthetic passes. So the 30-0 CV and 28-2 letter sweeps are grounding verdicts, not writing verdicts: on specificity the CV gap is 3.4 vs 3.8, on voice 2.6 vs 4.1 (4.6's bullets are padded, that part is fair).

Length: the rubric targets "a cover letter near 250 to 350 words" while the product spec is a 250-word body. 4.6 writes about 206 words, 5.5 about 245, and the judge reason on syn-14 says "Output 1 is somewhat short". The letter winner was the longer output in 31 of 32 passes (low) and 26 of 32 (medium). For CVs it was the shorter output 32 of 32, so the judge is not length-chasing overall; it is following a rubric that penalises 4.6's letters twice (fabrication and length).

My own read of four letters (syn-02, 07, 14, 16, 4.6 vs 5.5-medium): 5.5 wins syn-07 on prose and grounding; syn-14 and syn-02 go to 5.5 on grounding with 4.6 reading a touch smoother; syn-16 goes to 4.6 on German prose (5.5 has "Schriftwechsel auf Deutsch ist meine Muttersprache", a broken sentence, and "Claims-Team" lifted from the ad) and to 5.5 on grounding. On writing alone I would call it 1-1-2, not 28-2. The honest summary: 5.5 is clearly more grounded; it is not clearly a better writer.

Prompt confound: 5.5 ran on prompts with the em dashes, shouting and duplicate rules removed and a voice block replacing temperature; 4.6 ran production. F7 (10 kits vs 0) and F8 (23 vs 2) are at least partly prompt effects, and the CV grounding gap could be too (the variant widens the missing-skills scope to every JSON field). The run that settles it is cheap: **arm C, Sonnet 4.6 on the `sonnet55` prompt variant**, 16 synthetic kits, judged against both existing arms (about $5 generation, about $15 judge). If C closes most of the grounding gap, production should port the prompts regardless of the model. If C still loses the CV 0-16 on grounding, the model is the cause and the switch is justified on grounding alone.

A second style shift to decide on: 5.5 writes an explicit gap disclaimer in 11 to 12 of 16 letters (4.6: 4). syn-14 even says "I won't pretend to have 12-15 years in program management". Neither prompt asks for this. Honest, but a hiring manager reads it as a self-rejection. The production PR needs a line on how to handle gaps (transferable experience only, no "I haven't" sentences, or a limit of one).

## 3. Reliability: F12/F14/F18 root cause

Every 5.5 "invalid JSON" output (7 calls, all `stop_reason: end_turn`, 420 to 500 output tokens against `max_tokens` 4000 to 4096, so not truncation) has the same shape: a complete JSON object, then "Wait, the subject must be 60 characters or fewer. Corrected output:", then a second JSON object with a shorter subject. `parseStrictJsonObject` (`src/lib/agents/unified-writer-agent.ts:883`) matches `/\{[\s\S]*\}/`, which spans from the first brace to the last, so it swallows both objects and `JSON.parse` fails. F14 is the same event seen by the scorer. The trigger is the 60-character subject cap: 5.5 (literal instruction follower, thinking on) catches its own violation after emitting the JSON and self-corrects in the text block.

First-attempt validator misses on synthetic emails and follow-ups: 4.6 21 of 32 (12 are follow-ups over 80 words, 6 are subjects over 60), 5.5-low 14 of 32 (8 subjects over 60, 4 "over four sentences", 2 double JSON), medium 12 of 32. So 5.5 fails validation less often on the first try; it fails the retry differently (double JSON instead of a clean rewrite) and that is what turns into a missing document. The email "win" for 4.6 in the low comparison is largely these auto-losses: on judged pairs across all 30 kits 5.5-low wins email 14-10 and medium 18-10.

Not a production blocker, but the PR must include three fixes before any 5.5 traffic: (1) use `output_config.format` with a JSON schema for `write_email` and `write_follow_up_email` (and the other JSON tasks), which removes the trailing-prose failure class; (2) if structured output is not used, replace the greedy regex with a balanced-brace scan that takes the first complete object; (3) stop the model from hitting the cap in the first place: compute the subject in code (`${prefix}: ${shortTitle}, ${name}` with a title truncation rule) or raise the limit, since 9 of 16 first-attempt subjects were 61 to 79 characters because the ad title is long ("Senior Software Engineer, Backend - Payments Platform"). Also note the "four sentences" counter splits on every period, so "Google Analytics (basic)." style bodies miscount; that is a validator bug on both models.

## 4. Low vs medium

Head to head (30 kits, two passes) low vs medium: CV 15-10 low, letter 13-10 low, fit 21-6 low, email 17-8 medium, follow-up 13-9 medium, with 4 to 8 disagreeing passes per document. Medium is not better at writing; it is slightly better at the two short JSON tasks, and that advantage is mostly fewer retries (medium 9 synthetic email retries vs low 12). Cost: $0.214 vs $0.223 per kit. Latency at concurrency 4 on synthetic kits: low 40s per kit, medium 44s, 4.6 105s; per task 5.5 is 2 to 3x faster everywhere (optimize_cv 10s vs 33s, parse_cv 6s vs 20s). Fit scores: both 5.5 arms score every synthetic case 5 to 23 points higher than 4.6 (syn-02 bait: 46 vs 57 and 66, syn-13: 34 vs 57 and 62) on the unchanged `skill_matching` prompt. The Weak/Fair/Strong thresholds will shift, and medium inflates slightly more than low.

Recommendation: **low for every task.** Medium buys nothing measurable on the documents that matter and adds latency and score inflation. Revisit medium for the two email tasks only if structured output does not remove the retries.

## 5. Verdict

**Switch, with conditions, and not on the headline numbers.** The strongest evidence is not the 30-0 judge tables, which a strict rubric and tuned prompts inflate; it is that 5.5's CVs on synthetic cases contain no invented skills, titles or numbers while every 4.6 CV does, that 5.5 is 2 to 3x faster at 23% lower cost, and that its emails pass the validator more often on the first try. The weak evidence: writing quality (my read is even), the fit assessment (grounding 1.3 to 1.6 for both arms, both list JD requirements as matched), the email comparison (confounded by the double-JSON bug), and anything about latency at concurrency 1 (not run for 4.6 on synthetic cases). No second judge has run.

Conditions for the production PR:

1. Run arm C (4.6 on the `sonnet55` prompts, synthetic cases) before merging, and port the prompt changes to production whatever it shows. Three of them (trailing comma in the CV schema, "Job Copilot" name, dangling "Rule 4d") are plain bugs.
2. Structured output (`output_config.format`) on every JSON task, or at minimum a balanced-brace parser, plus a code-built email subject. Verify by replaying the 7 failing raw outputs through the new parser.
3. Effort low, adaptive thinking, no temperature, `max_tokens` as in `evals/lib/arms.ts`.
4. A gap-handling rule in the letter and email prompts (no "I haven't" or "won't pretend" sentences; transferable experience only).
5. Recalibrate fit thresholds against the 5.5 score distribution (about +10 points on the same inputs) or expect more "Fair Fit" on weak matches.
6. Keep the `skill_matching` prompt on the list for the next eval: it marks bait requirements claimable on both models and feeds both the fit text and the letter allow-list.
7. Watch in production: 5.5's availability and start-date lines in emails (the one fabrication class it has), and the retry rate per email task after the parser change.
